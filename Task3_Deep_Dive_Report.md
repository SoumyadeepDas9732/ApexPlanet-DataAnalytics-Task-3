## 3. Core Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)

Five core KPIs were selected to monitor overall business performance.

### KPI 1: Total Revenue

**Formula:**

Total Revenue = Sum of Total Sales

**Result:** ₹156,004,434.05

**Business Rationale:**

Total Revenue represents the total sales value generated by the
business and provides an overall measure of sales performance.

---

### KPI 2: Total Orders

**Formula:**

Total Orders = Count of Unique Order IDs

**Result:** 992 Orders

**Business Rationale:**

Total Orders measures the overall transaction volume and helps
understand the level of purchasing activity in the business.

---

### KPI 3: Average Order Value (AOV)

**Formula:**

Average Order Value = Total Revenue / Total Orders

**Result:** ₹157,262.53

**Business Rationale:**

Average Order Value measures the average amount of revenue generated
per order. It helps the business understand the typical value of
each transaction.

---

### KPI 4: Unique Customers

**Formula:**

Unique Customers = Count of Unique Customer IDs

**Result:** 947 Customers

**Business Rationale:**

This KPI represents the number of individual customers contributing
to business activity and provides an indication of the overall
customer base.

---

### KPI 5: Average Revenue per Customer

**Formula:**

Average Revenue per Customer = Total Revenue / Unique Customers

**Result:** ₹164,735.41

**Business Rationale:**

Average Revenue per Customer measures the average revenue contribution
of each customer and helps evaluate overall customer value.

## 4. Deep-Dive Analysis: Customer Segmentation

Customer segmentation was selected as the primary deep-dive area for
Task 3.

Customers were aggregated at the customer level using the following
metrics:

- Total Revenue
- Total Orders
- Total Quantity
- Average Order Value

Customers were then segmented according to their total revenue
contribution.

Revenue quartiles were used to create three customer segments.

### High Value

Customers whose total revenue was greater than or equal to the
75th percentile were classified as High Value customers.

### Medium Value

Customers whose total revenue was between the median and the
75th percentile were classified as Medium Value customers.

### Low Value

Customers whose total revenue was below the median were classified
as Low Value customers.

This segmentation provides a simple business-oriented method for
identifying customers according to their financial contribution to
the business.

---

## 5. Customer Segment Analysis

The customer segmentation analysis was used to examine:

- Number of customers in each segment
- Revenue generated by each segment
- Average customer spending
- Highest-value customers
- Customer purchasing behavior

The revenue generated by the customer segments was:

| Customer Segment | Revenue |
|---|---:|
| High Value | ₹81,219,576.59 |
| Medium Value | ₹42,243,676.77 |
| Low Value | ₹32,541,180.69 |

### Key Finding

**Highest revenue customer segment:** High Value

**Revenue generated by High Value customers:** ₹81,219,576.59

**Number of unique High Value customers:** 246

The High Value segment generated approximately **52.1% of the total
business revenue** while representing only about **26% of unique
customers**.

This is an important business finding because it shows that a
relatively small proportion of customers contributes a
disproportionately large share of overall revenue.

As a result, retaining and strengthening relationships with High
Value customers can be particularly important for maintaining
business revenue.
